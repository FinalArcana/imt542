Hi! Really enjoyed your presentation. You did a great job explaining the nuances of AO3's structure. It's genuinely hard to communicate how these fandom platforms work to people who aren't familiar with them, so I think you did really well there. The reasoning behind the current AO3 data structure's limitations also came through clearly.

I also appreciated that you considered the RSS feed approach before landing on webscraping. Walking through why it fell short, like only going back 25 entries and missing several engagement metadata showed that your design decisions were well thought out and that you tried other ways before landing on one specific direction.

From a FAIR perspective, the accessibility issues with AO3 really stand out. There's no straightforward way to access fanwork metadata, and the bulk download process being slow all make the case for why this project is needed in the first place. That came through well in the presentation.

One thing that would've made it stronger is showing the improved structure in action with some actual charts or visualizations. I know that seems to be in the plans, so I'm looking forward to seeing that come together.

On the quality control side, it could use some more concrete methods. I also think the data structure could benefit from more consistency metadata. For example, recording the scrape timestamp, scraper version, login status during scraping, and expected versus actual works captured would go a long way. Login status in particular feels important since locked or mature works may be inaccessible depending on the session, which could silently affect how complete your dataset actually is.


Overall, I think this project has solid potential. As someone who uses AO3, I've always seen those fic and ship rankings floating around and wondered how people actually pulled that data. Your project really gained insight into how one might go about getting that information. Looking forward to seeing where this goes.
